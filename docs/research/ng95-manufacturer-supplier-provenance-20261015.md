# Second-generation 9-5: corporate manufacturer and supplier provenance

Research date: 2026-10-15. **Distinguish corporate ownership, vehicle manufacturer/assembler, platform developer and actual tier-1 component manufacturer.** These are different fields; a change in ownership does not establish that every installed component manufacturer changed.

## Dated production and control facts

1. GM's original [2010 Saab VIN card](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/dealerworld/vincards/pdf/2010%20Saab%20VIN%20Card.pdf) explicitly identifies **9-5 (650)** assembly plant code **4 = Trollhättan, Sweden**. This is manufacturer-origin documentation dated 2009-10-28 and does **not** certify a specific 2011 VIN or every component.
2. In [Saab's February 2010 announcement](https://saabworld.net/wp/final-first-generation-saab-9-5-produced/), the **final original-generation 9-5** had rolled off the Trollhättan line on 2010-02-01, with the replacement 9-5 prepared there.
3. [Saab's 2010-year-end sales statement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/saab-automobile-sees-ongoing-momentum-in-2010-sales-113068664.html) describes Saab as an independent automaker following the **Spyker** acquisition and a seven-week production hiatus at Trollhättan in early 2010.
4. [Saab's May 2011 restart release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/saab-automobile-restarts-production-122719668.html) establishes that Saab Automobile restarted its Trollhättan production after a 2011 stoppage, initially building approximately 100 vehicles, including a 9-5 Aero XWD.
5. The 650's **GM Epsilon II** engineering lineage makes GM-family cross-reference research worthwhile, but **does not prove** shared GM/Opel/Buick donor parts, hardware revisions or ECU compatibility. Exact assembly supplier, OE part number, VIN/build application, geometry, connectors, firmware and official supersession direction are needed.

## Required distinct provenance fields (future component records)

| Field | Appropriate observation |
|---|---|
| Vehicle brand | Saab |
| Original car manufacturer / VIN legal identity | Verify original Saab Automobile records and the actual candidate's manufacturer label |
| Assembly facility and date | Trollhättan, exact VIN/build evidence if obtainable |
| Owner at development / ownership at build | GM development-period control vs Spyker-owned Saab Automobile production period, per actual build date |
| Platform architectural origin | GM Epsilon II engineering; **not** a blanket parts-interchange authorization |
| Component OE manufacturer | **Unknown until part marking, factory EPC or supplier evidence** |
| Actual part number / revision | From installed label and Saab WebEPC/manufacturer parts chain |
| Other brands sharing the component | Candidate only pending source-confirmed cross-reference, fit and function checks |
| Replacement quality/durability advantage | Unknown until supported measurements/aging evidence |

## Implications for our Saab research

- Investigate **Opel/Vauxhall Insignia, Buick Regal/LaCrosse, other GM Epsilon II** as *leads*, not approved donor inventories. Components may vary in dimensions, trim, wiring, BCM/ECM software, safety, fuel/emissions and original equipment suppliers.
- Keep **Saab-designed features**, GM platform architecture and tier-one supplied assemblies distinct rather than claiming the whole second generation became "GM-manufactured" or "Spyker-manufactured."
- For a 2011 parts car, original **VIN, build month, equipment codes and actual installed part label** determine whether a cross-brand candidate warrants more research.
- Separate engine A20NFT/LHU/GM-family service channels, AF40/Aisin transmission maker, and module vendor where evidenced. Do not turn one manufacturer's marketing name into a proven drop-in swap.
- This is provenance research, **not** a claim that the second-generation 9-5 is more or less reliable due to corporate ownership.
